EVERY EFFECT IN CODE

I analysed a real TikTok edit and measured the effects in it. This page rebuilds each one as a scroll-driven web effect — parallax, band reveals, whip pans, flash cuts and split-tone grading.

01 — Parallax

Depth is just speed.

Four layers, four scroll rates. The background moves at 12% of scroll, the subject at 62%, the foreground haze at 92%. Your eye reads the difference as distance.

GSAP ScrollTrigger scrub: 1, one tween per layer, driven off a Lenis-smoothed scroll value.

02 — Band reveal

The signature move.

A horizontal band opens mid-shot and swaps the background behind the subject, while she stays continuous across the edge. That continuity is the tell: she's a separate masked layer, not part of either plate.

Two stacked plates + an animated clip-path: inset(). The cutout sits above both, so it never clips.

03 — Whip pan

Blur sells the speed.

The detector found 19 whip pans in 16 seconds — each one a spike in global motion paired with a collapse in sharpness. Horizontal travel plus a directional blur that scales with velocity.

04 — Colour grade

Cool shadows,
warm highlights.

Split toning is measurable: compare red-minus-blue in the shadows against the highlights. In the source clip shadows sat at R−B −19 and highlights at +16. Scroll to drive the grade.

Two blend layers — multiply teal on the lows, screen amber on the highs — plus contrast and saturation.

05 — Rhythm

Cut on the beat.

100% of cuts in the source landed within 120 ms of an audio onset, at 177 BPM. Here the scroll position is the transport — each beat marker triggers a hard cut and a flash.
